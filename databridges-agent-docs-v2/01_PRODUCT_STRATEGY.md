# Product and Commercial Strategy

## Strategic objective

Use the website to generate qualified conversations for services DataBridges can deliver credibly and profitably. Traffic, tools and content are supporting mechanisms, not outcomes.

## Market hypotheses

These statements are hypotheses until supported by interviews, sales evidence or conversion data.

### Primary audience

Irish small and medium organisations, especially professional-services and operational teams, that:

- have recurring administrative or information-heavy work;
- are considering AI, automation or Microsoft-platform improvements;
- lack a clear way to prioritise opportunities and risks;
- value practical implementation over generic transformation advice.

### Secondary audience

Public-sector or regulated teams seeking training, discovery or governance support where procurement, employer obligations and conflicts are handled explicitly.

### Likely buyers

- owner or managing director;
- operations lead;
- digital or transformation lead;
- marketing lead for visibility work;
- learning, governance or compliance sponsor.

Do not write as if all of these buyers share one journey or problem.

## Customer problems

The website should test whether buyers respond to these problems:

1. potential customers cannot find or understand the organisation online;
2. staff spend time on avoidable administration and fragmented workflows;
3. leaders cannot distinguish useful AI opportunities from tool noise;
4. an implementation needs governance, adoption or training to succeed;
5. claims about AI value lack evidence and operational ownership.

## Positioning

> DataBridges helps organisations find practical improvements, implement the right technology, and keep human judgement where it matters.

Supporting promise:

> Diagnose before automating. Prove before claiming. Govern while building.

Avoid a broad promise to “grow the business” when the offer is specifically website visibility.

## Offer architecture

### Entry offers

| Offer | Buyer problem | Expected output | Validation signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visibility Review or Sprint | Weak search/AI-search visibility | Prioritised findings, fixes and baseline | Qualified enquiry or paid pilot |
| AI and Automation Opportunity Map | Unclear operational opportunities | Ranked opportunities, exclusions, risks and next steps | Paid discovery and implementation demand |
| Training or governance workshop | Adoption or control gap | Role-specific capability or control plan | Follow-on adoption work and participant outcomes |

Names, duration, scope and pricing require explicit commercial approval before publication.

### Delivery capabilities

- workflow and process improvement;
- responsible automation and AI augmentation;
- Microsoft 365, SharePoint and Power Platform work where appropriate;
- applied data and forecasting work with explicit limitations;
- governance and training throughout discovery and implementation.

## Non-linear customer paths

Examples:

```text
Visibility review -> website improvement -> broader opportunity discovery
Training -> governance review -> controlled implementation
Opportunity Map -> process redesign -> automation -> training
Governance need -> policy/control work -> approved pilot
```

No page should imply that governance happens only after automation.

## Differentiation to test

- willingness to recommend elimination or process change instead of software;
- human-centred automation decisions;
- evidence and limitations shown in public proof;
- ability to connect business diagnosis with implementation and training;
- practical experience across operational, Microsoft and applied-AI work.

These are positioning hypotheses, not superiority claims.

## Commercial measurements

Establish a baseline before redesigning. Review monthly once volume permits.

| Stage | Primary measure |
|---|---|
| Discovery | qualified visits to service and proof pages |
| Intent | enquiry, booking and resource-completion rate |
| Qualification | percentage of leads matching the target audience/problem |
| Sales | discovery calls, proposals and wins by source |
| Economics | delivery days, gross margin and sales effort by offer |
| Trust | claim corrections, unsubscribes and evidence-review failures |

Raw traffic is not the primary success metric.

## Minimum evidence before expansion

Before funding a custom lead platform, public index or large directory, require:

- at least one clear target segment and buyer;
- repeatable evidence of the problem through interviews, enquiries or delivery;
- an approved offer with delivery scope and commercial owner;
- a measurable conversion path;
- maintenance capacity and a named owner;
- a documented stop condition.

Numeric thresholds should be set by the owner after current baselines are measured. Do not invent them in advance.

## Explicit exclusions

- guaranteed rankings, AI recommendations or financial return;
- legal advice or compliance certification;
- autonomous replacement of accountable professional judgement;
- public benchmark claims based on convenience samples;
- product development undertaken only because it is technically interesting.

## Quarterly strategy review

Record:

1. which audience and problem produced qualified work;
2. which offer converted and remained deliverable;
3. which assumptions failed;
4. which service or content should be simplified, paused or removed;
5. whether any experimental product has earned further investment.
