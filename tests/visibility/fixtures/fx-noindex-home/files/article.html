Year-end checklist for small businesses

By Aoife Brennan, 4 November 2025

The end of the financial year is the best moment to tidy a small business's records. A short, repeatable checklist turns a stressful scramble into an hour or two of steady work. Here is the list we follow with our own clients.

Nothing on this list needs special software. A spreadsheet, your bank statements and an hour of focus are usually enough to get started, and most of the steps are quicker if you keep your records up to date during the year.

Why a year-end checklist matters

A checklist keeps the same steps in the same order every year, which reduces errors and surprises. It also gives your accountant clean records, which usually lowers their fee.

Reconcile every bank account

Match each bank, card and loan statement to your accounting records for the final month. Investigate any difference, however small, before you move on to the next step.

Check payroll and PAYE records

Confirm that every payslip and Revenue submission agrees with your payroll reports. Fix any mismatch now, while the details are fresh.

  • All employee details are up to date
  • Each pay run was submitted on time
  • PAYE and USC payments match the reports
  • Benefits in kind are recorded
  • Leavers have their final pay processed

Chase unpaid invoices

Send a polite reminder today.

Most late payers respond to a short, friendly message that restates the invoice number, the amount and the agreed due date. Keep a note of every reminder you send, because a clear record makes any later conversation about a bad debt much simpler.

Review your stock and assets

Count your stock and update your fixed asset register, recording anything that was sold, scrapped or replaced during the year. Photographs and dated notes make this easier to review later.

Plan ahead for tax

Once the books are reconciled you can estimate your tax bill for the year. Set money aside so that the payment never comes as a shock. Check the official preliminary tax guidance for the dates and rules that apply to your business. Put those dates in your calendar now. If you are unsure which rules apply, ask your accountant before the year closes. A short conversation in December is far cheaper than a penalty in the following autumn.
