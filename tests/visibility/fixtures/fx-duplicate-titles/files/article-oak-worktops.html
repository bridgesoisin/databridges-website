Choosing oak worktops for a busy kitchen

By Aoife Nolan, 14 July 2026

Oak is the most popular timber for solid wood worktops, and for good reason. It is strong, it ages gracefully and it can be sanded and refinished many times during its long life.

Why choose oak for a worktop?

Oak is hard enough to resist daily knocks and forgiving enough to repair when it is marked. Its open grain also takes oil well, which keeps the surface warm to the touch.

How thick should a solid oak worktop be?

Most kitchens suit a thickness of 38 millimetres, which looks substantial without overwhelming the cabinets. Islands and breakfast bars can take 40 millimetres or more if you want a bolder edge.

Does oak need to be sealed?

Oak should be treated with a hardwax oil or a similar finish before use, especially around the sink and hob. A good finish protects the timber from water without hiding the grain.

How often should you re-oil it?

Re-oil a busy worktop every six to twelve months, or when water stops beading on the surface. Sand out any marks first, and read the FSC guidance if you are choosing a certified board.
