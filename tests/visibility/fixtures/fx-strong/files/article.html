AI for small business: where to start

By Aoife Brennan | 12 August 2026 | Updated 15 September 2026

Most owners we meet want to use AI but do not know where to begin. This guide sets out a simple route that starts small, keeps people in charge and shows results within a month.

Why start small?

A small first project teaches your team how the tools behave without risking anything important. It also gives you a clear before and after, which makes the next decision much easier.

Think of it as a pilot. If it works, you have proof and a template, and if it does not, you have lost a few hours rather than a budget.

What counts as a boring task?

A boring task is repetitive, follows a clear rule and does not need empathy or judgement. Typical examples are chasing approvals, copying figures between systems and filing documents.

  • Entering the same details into two systems
  • Sending routine reminders by hand
  • Sorting incoming forms into folders

How do you pick the first process?

Choose a process that happens often, hurts when it goes wrong and involves no sensitive decisions. Ask the people who do it, because they usually know exactly where the time disappears.

Which tools do small firms need?

Most small firms already own what they need inside their existing Microsoft or Google subscription. Start there, and only buy something new when you can show that the current tools fall short.

What should you keep human?

Keep anything that involves a difficult conversation, a hiring choice or a judgement about a person. The European Commission offers a helpful guide to AI rules for small firms if you want to check your obligations.

How do you measure the result?

Count the hours spent on the task for a month before the change and a month after it. If the saving is real, move on to the next process, and if it is not, stop and learn why.

Write the numbers down and share them with the team. People are far more willing to adopt a new way of working when they can see the time it gives back.
